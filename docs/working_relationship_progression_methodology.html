Working-Relationship Progression

A design methodology — replacing the pattern-derived M1–M5 maturity model with a per-activity working-relationship classification, a three-axis readiness gate, and a human-impact propagation pathway
Tim King & Claude · September 2026 · Working Document
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress
Status — Concept Stage, Not Yet Adopted Framework-Wide

This is a working synthesis of the methodology this session has converged on — not a locked framework spec. It replaces the pattern-derived M1–M5 maturity model with a per-activity working-relationship classification, a three-axis readiness gate, and a human-impact propagation pathway. It is validated so far against three pilots spanning all three role tiers — HRBP Manager / PCF 7.4.4 (Manage employee grievances, Managerial tier), Recruiter / PCF 7.2.3.4 (Select and reject candidates, Operational tier), and CHRO / PCF 7.1.1.2 (Define HR and business function roles and accountability, Executive tier) — and is written to precede, not substitute for, the pilot findings summary for GiGi Garcia.

1. Why This Layer Exists

The framework’s original M1–M5 maturity model derives a single H% figure from an activity’s pattern type (Decision, Knowledge, Document, Transaction, Exception), applying a fixed decay curve shared by every activity of that pattern. Two findings broke that assumption. First, at the activity level: 7.2.1.3 (Approve job requisition, pattern Decision, M3=80) and 7.2.4.3 (Hire candidate, pattern Transaction, M3=35) carry opposite pattern-derived numbers yet collapse to the same real working relationship — Oversight, a human checkpoint on each instance — once classified by what actually happens rather than the pattern label. Second, at the role level: two roles can land at nearly identical H% and still retain completely different work — GiGi Garcia’s review of the role-level dashboard showed HRBP Manager and Director of HR Ops converging on a similar M3 band while retaining judgment/coaching/conflict work versus exceptions/controls/compliance work respectively, with very different human consequence.

The pattern-derived model measures human presence. This methodology is the first attempt at measuring what stays true about the working relationship itself — who does the work, who owns the outcome, and what has to be true for the human share of it to succeed.

2. The Working-Relationship Ramp

Six ordinal categories replace pattern type as the source of an activity’s H%/maturity value, read left to right as increasing automation:

CategoryWhat It Means
Human-onlyNo meaningful AI role, by design — legal, ethical, or relational reasons hold regardless of maturity level.
JudgmentA person makes the actual call. AI’s role is informational only — it can supply data or a ranking, but the reasoning and decision are the person’s.
OversightAI performs or proposes the work; a person reviews and approves each instance before it takes effect.
AugmentationA person remains the primary actor and does the work; AI assists — drafts, suggests, summarizes — but doesn’t act on its own.
Agent-delegationAn AI agent performs the work autonomously within policy bounds; a person handles only flagged exceptions, not every instance.
AutomationAI performs and completes the work with no person in the loop for that instance.

Progression moves toward Automation one adjacent step at a time (skipping a category is not modeled). The ramp is ordinal, not categorical — it carries a validated single-hue, monotone-lightness color sequence (Sept 10, 2026) distinct from the five work-pattern colors, which keep their separate job as visual/structural identity (badges, pattern-mix bars, capability-map cards) and are not being replaced by this ramp.

3. Current State and Progression Ceiling — replacing M1–M5

The five-level M1–M5 curve is retired for any activity classified on the working-relationship ramp. In its place, each classified activity carries two values:

TermDefinition
Current StateThe activity’s real working-relationship category today.
Progression CeilingThe ceiling category this specific activity can plausibly reach, for technical or governance reasons. May be undecided/open. May equal Current State — i.e. no further runway either because the activity is already fully automated or because it is already at its human-side ceiling.

An earlier version of this model also tracked a third value, Future State (a near-term target sitting between Current State and the ceiling). It's been dropped — Current State and Progression Ceiling carry the model on their own, and a separate near-term-target field added no decision either one didn't already make.

Five worked examples from PCF 7.1/7.2/7.4 — spanning all three role tiers now that Recruiter and CHRO have been piloted alongside HRBP Manager — show why Current State and Progression Ceiling are not always the same value, and why M1/M2 never needed to “mean” anything once the five-point curve is gone:

4. Granularity: Where Each Kind of Readiness Lives

Not every part of this methodology needs to operate at the same level of detail, and forcing uniform granularity was the wrong instinct. The activity’s own classification (Current State / Progression Ceiling) has to live at L4 — or at L3 when the process is a genuine APQC leaf with no L4 children, confirmed against the real APQC PCF Cross-Industry v8.0 source file (18 such leaf L3s exist domain-wide in PCF 7.0, including all five L3s under 7.4 Manage Employee Relations — a real structural fact, not a missing build). Technical/process readiness (below) has to live at the same granularity, because it is evidence about AI performance on one specific piece of work.

Person, Manager, Team, and Accountability readiness, by contrast, are properties of a human or an organizational unit, not of an activity — a manager does not have a different capacity to absorb change per L4. Those signals consume an aggregate of the L4-level change underneath them (magnitude, velocity, concentration — the kind of rollup the existing Transition Magnitude Index already performs) rather than needing L4 granularity themselves.

5. The Readiness Gate

An activity advances from Current State toward its Progression Ceiling only when three independent signals clear — not one role-level score standing in for all three:

AxisWhat It MeasuresGranularityInstrument
Technical / ProcessWhether AI performance on this specific activity supports the specific transition being attempted.Per L4 (or leaf-L3), per transition.New — the per-transition evidence ladder below (§6). No existing instrument anywhere in the framework.
Role-levelWhether the person(s) actually doing the work — read off the Responsible role(s) via RACI, not only Accountable — have the authority, confidence, and psychological safety to exercise it. Multiple touching roles: weakest-link, not averaged.Per role, via its RACI touches on the L3/L4.Not yet defined. Deliberately not the existing AI Transition Pathways self-assessment / TMI tier — per Tim (Sept 15, 2026), that instrument stays in GiGi Garcia’s own review lane, not this sandbox. The leading candidate is GiGi’s own Human Readiness/Agency, Human Criticality, and Human Intensity work (§9), which she is developing directly against the three pilot roles below.
Organizational-levelBroader climate — trust, psychological safety, leadership readiness, change capacity. Not tied to any one activity.Domain- or company-wide; closer to a go/no-go condition than a per-activity gate.No existing instrument. Distinct from Role-level per GiGi’s reflections, not a duplicate of it.

6. The Per-Transition Evidence Ladder (Technical / Process Axis)

The five adjacent transitions on the ramp are not interchangeable — each represents a different delegation of authority and needs a different kind of evidence. Volume of real instances is a precondition for trusting any of these numbers, not a competing metric alongside them.

TransitionEvidence Needed
Human-only → JudgmentRelevance/usefulness of AI-supplied input — no autonomous action exists yet, so there is no error rate to measure.
Judgment → OversightAgreement rate between the AI-proposed action and what the human would have decided, trustworthy only once enough instances have run.
Oversight → AugmentationAudit-pass rate — a high share of full-review instances passing clean, sustained over volume, justifying sampled review over full review.
Augmentation → Agent-delegationAccuracy/error rate within defined policy bounds, plus exception-detection recall — can the system reliably flag when it is out of bounds.
Agent-delegation → AutomationSustained low exception rate over real volume, plus an explicit governance sign-off gate layered on top — this step can be a policy choice, not only a technical threshold (see the job-requisition example above).

7. The Human Impact Pathway

The original Shock Wave Effect model (five rings: Role, Person, Manager, Team, Accountability) is extended, per GiGi Garcia’s reflections, into seven stages, with one consistent set of questions applied at every stage rather than a bespoke instrument per ring: What changed? Who absorbs it? Where did the work go? What new risk appeared? What new human capability is required? Who now owns the outcome? The pathway’s trigger (Work Change) is inherently activity-level; downstream stages read off that change as it aggregates outward.

StageFirst Real Finding (7.4.4 — Manage employee grievances)
Work ChangeAI absorbs the administrative shell (case intake, precedent lookup, documentation); the determination itself stays entirely human.
Role ImpactOne of ≥ 9 L3s HRBP Manager holds Accountable for; this one’s curve is unusually flat next to others that plausibly progress further — a compositional shift in the role’s real time allocation that a flat role-level H% would not show.
Human ImpactIntensity likely rises even if volume doesn’t (validating AI’s prep, absorbing the harder tail); Criticality is already high (litigation/executive/regulatory escalation triggers exist in the real GDP); Agency is unmeasured — nothing tracks whether HRBP Manager can actually override a bad AI-surfaced precedent.
Manager AbsorptionThe real functional escalation path names Director of HR Ops, not VP People & Culture (the provisional reportsTo edge) — a second, independent argument for correcting that edge, beyond the original RACI self-consistency check.
Team EffectUnmeasured. No data exists on whether HR Generalist or others absorb any freed capacity around this role.
AccountabilityAlready fully concentrated, not an emerging risk — HRBP Manager holds 100% of the Accountable share across the entire real M1→M5 curve for this activity.
Org OutcomeIf “AI redistributes work, not accountability” holds, administrative capacity grows while risk-bearing judgment stays exactly as concentrated — readiness investment has to target this one role, not a widening pool.

8. Second and Third Pilots — Recruiter and CHRO

GiGi Garcia’s recommendation was to test the methodology against roles other than HRBP Manager before trusting it. Rather than pick two more Managerial-tier roles, this pilot deliberately spans the org’s vertical range: Recruiter (Operational tier, the narrowest and highest-volume Accountable footprint of the three) and CHRO (Executive tier, the domain’s only Exec-Accountable L3) — testing whether the Human Impact Pathway and the ceiling logic hold at both ends of the hierarchy, not only in the middle.

Recruiter — First Real Finding (7.2.3.4, Select and reject candidates)

Recruiter holds Accountable for 7.2.2 (Recruit/Source candidates), 7.2.3 (Screen and select candidates), and 7.2.4 (Manage new hire/re-hire) — the highest-stakes, highest-volume middle of the Recruit-to-Hire pipeline, bounded on either side by HRBP Manager (7.2.1, requisitions) and HRIS Analyst (7.2.5, applicant records). 7.2.3.4 is the single highest-consequence L4 in the entire 7.2 pilot (Judgment, H% 82, Critical) and sits squarely inside Recruiter’s own Accountable scope — the natural choice for this pilot.

Reproduced or diverged? Both, in different ways. Recruiter substantially reproduces 7.4.4’s shape — concentrated single-role Accountability, unmeasured Agency, an intensity-not-volume shift, the same “redistributes work, not accountability” outcome — but diverges on two counts: its reportsTo edge needed no correction (Director of TA was already strongly evidenced), and its Role Impact finding comes from spread across its own 3 L3s rather than one flat L3 sitting next to others. CHRO diverges more substantially: the narrowest Accountable scope of the three, no Manager Absorption ring to apply (top of catalog), a domain-wide rather than nearby Team Effect, and — most importantly — a taxonomy result that confirms the pattern-derived intuition instead of overturning it, because strategy-formation work was already understood as human-heavy before this method existed. The methodology held across all three tiers without modification, but it does not produce the same kind of finding at every tier, and treating “reproduces 7.4.4” as the success condition would have been the wrong bar.

9. Known Open Constructs — Named, Not Yet Built

Human Intensity (cognitive/emotional/relational/operational demand) is genuinely new — nothing today distinguishes “less human involvement” from “the human involvement that remains is more demanding.” Human Criticality substantially overlaps the four-tier consequence-of-error tag already built into the Sept 9–10 L4 working-relationship taxonomy pilot — mostly a naming/formalizing exercise, not a new instrument. Human Agency (authority, information, capacity, confidence, skill, psychological safety) sharpens Person-ring readiness into something functional rather than sentiment-based; psychological safety specifically functions as an AI governance/control variable — whether a person will actually challenge a wrong AI recommendation — not a culture metric. Zero-R/A-touch roles may hold real, invisible relational/leadership work (influencing, coaching, sense-making, trust-building) that a RACI-based framework structurally cannot see — flagged as a genuine blind spot, not resolved by anything in this methodology.

Sept 2026 update: GiGi Garcia’s own working reply on the three pilots confirms this is squarely her track. She names Human Readiness/Agency (“can the person actually exercise the judgment they are being held accountable for?”), Human Criticality (“what happens if the decision is wrong, who is affected, and how reversible is the damage?”), and Human Intensity (“does AI reduce work, or does it concentrate the hardest parts of the work?”) as exactly what she wants to go deeper on next, and has started a role-by-role deep dive on the three prototype positions to do it — asking, per role, what work leaves, what remains human, whether the remaining work intensifies, what judgment/authority/accountability shifts, and whether the role is being elevated, expanded, compressed, converged, or redesigned. She also names a fourth, closely related construct — Role Transition (what changes in skill, judgment, authority, identity, relationships, and accountability) — which is the same territory this document’s Role Impact and Human Impact stages (§7) already probe, but from the role’s side rather than the activity’s. None of these are built instruments yet; this note records where the next instrument for the Role-level axis (§5) is most likely to come from, and whose work it is.

10. Sourcing, Validation, and What This Document Deliberately Leaves Out

The real APQC Process Classification Framework Cross-Industry v8.0 source spreadsheet (obtained directly from APQC, not framework-authored) independently confirms the framework’s already-corrected PCF 7.0 counts — exactly 37 L3s and 100 L4s — and supplies the real element-description text used above for 7.4.4. It is now the ground truth for resolving any future “is this a leaf or a gap” question across all 13 domains, not only HR.

The Organizational Role Taxonomy / reportsTo practical sanity check (GiGi Garcia’s reflections, section 16) is a deliberately separate, parallel review track — not merged into this methodology — though its findings feed the Manager Absorption stage above directly, and the 7.4.4 pilot produced independent evidence for it.

The Recruiter and CHRO pilots (Section 8) are now complete — GiGi’s recommended test has been run. Left open by design, newly surfaced by those two pilots: whether the Role-level readiness axis’s “weakest-link across multiple touching roles” rule needs a distinct treatment for an Executive-tier, single-decision-maker L3 like CHRO’s, where no second touching role exists to be the weak link; and whether the Human Impact Pathway’s Manager Absorption stage needs an explicit top-of-catalog variant rather than defaulting to “no data” when a role has no manager to absorb into. Still open from before: what the technical-readiness instrument should concretely measure and collect at scale; and whether Human Intensity ultimately becomes its own tracked field or stays a qualitative note alongside Current State / Progression Ceiling.
