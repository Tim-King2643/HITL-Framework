Skills & Competencies

Grounded in Josh Bersin's “Full-Stack HR” model (The Great Reinvention of Human Resources Has Begun, January 2026) — the specific areas he identifies as where HR business teams focus once tactical/administrative work is automated.

  • Talent advisory — deciding who to hire, move, or promote, not just processing the request
  • Strategic talent management — identifying high-potential leaders and building career pathways aligned to both business and employee goals
  • Culture and engagement — building a culture of safety, innovation, and high performance
  • Organization and job design — adapting structure and roles as AI reshapes how work gets done
  • AI orchestration — building, stitching together, and architecting the AI agents that automate HR processes; a genuinely new skill area, not a rebrand of an old one

Framework connection: These five areas map cleanly onto the Role Impact Snapshot's finding — HRBP Manager's Knowledge-pattern dominance is exactly the kind of judgment-heavy, cross-functional work Bersin describes as what remains and grows for HR business teams as automation absorbs the tactical layer.

How the Role Has Evolved

HR Business Partner has existed as a defined role for 25+ years, originally specified as “HR Generalist,” per Bersin's June 2024 podcast on the role's transformation. What's changed is the center of gravity: Bersin's research finds 30–40% of existing HR job content is now automatable with relatively low effort — concentrated in exactly the tactical/administrative work the Getting Started stage lists. As that work is absorbed, the standard employee-to-HR-staff ratio (historically around 100:1) is shifting toward 200:1, 300:1, or higher, not because HR is shrinking, but because its content is changing. HR job postings have grown roughly 60% over five years even as the routine layer is automated — the profession isn't disappearing, its center of gravity is moving toward exactly the Knowledge-pattern, judgment-heavy work the Role Impact Snapshot shows HRBP Manager already dominated by.

A new, real risk worth flagging: Bersin identifies data quality, explainability, and AI bias as HR's newly-owned responsibility as automation scales — citing active litigation against HR technology vendors as evidence this isn't theoretical. This is a genuine candidate for a future Transition Trigger (the People-side analog of a Governed Decision Point): a threshold condition for when a role's AI-orchestration authority needs a defined escalation path, not just training.
