Organizational Role Taxonomy — PCF 8.0

The People-side counterpart to PCF — a normalized role catalog for PCF 8.0 (Manage Information Technology)

The People View of this framework mirrors the Process View's PCF structure with a role-based lens: instead of asking how AI reshapes a value stream, it asks how AI reshapes the specific job of the person doing the work. The taxonomy below is the anchor for that lens — a normalized, three-tier role catalog aligned to APQC PCF v8.0, scoped here to Domain 8.0 (Manage Information Technology), the same domain covered by every Value Stream Profile in the Process View. See also the PCF 7.0 (HR) taxonomy.

Reference Baseline, Not a Binding Structure

This taxonomy is a normalized cross-industry baseline. Role titles, reporting lines, and tier assignments should be adapted to reflect each organization's grading structure and nomenclature. Tiers reflect accountability levels, not compensation grades.

Three Tiers

Executive (E)

C-suite executives, SVPs, and VPs holding strategic accountability for domain outcomes, enterprise resource allocation, and board-level reporting.

Managerial (M)

Directors, Senior Managers, and Managers with operational ownership of domain-specific functions, team leadership, and cross-functional coordination.

Operational (O)

Specialists, Analysts, Coordinators, and Associates responsible for day-to-day execution, delivery, and process-level activity.

On the Reporting Structure

The reportsTo edges above are a first-pass, reasoned structure for the 15-role IT catalog, now checked two independent ways. First, a self-consistency pass cross-referenced each role's claimed boss against who actually shows up as Consulted/Informed on that role's Accountable-owned processes in the real RACI data — 2 of 14 edges show strong support, 3 moderate, 5 weak or ambiguous, 2 show no support in the RACI signal, and 4 have no data at all since those Operational-tier roles never hold Accountable. Second, the weak and zero-support edges were checked against real, named external sources on typical IT reporting structures (Gartner, PwC, Databricks, Built In, TOGAF's own positioning, and others). External sourcing resolved two internal ties in favor of the drafted edge (Enterprise Architect→CIO, Director of Data & Analytics→Chief Data Officer), and independently confirmed why CISO→CIO and Chief Data Officer→CIO were the weakest internal edges: PwC's 2026 research found 72% of CISOs now report directly to the CEO or board (up from 35% in 2018), and Gartner's CDO research puts only 16% of CDOs reporting to the CIO versus 30% direct to CEO. Both of those are already flagged context-dependent above, and both checks now agree on why. Three edges (Systems Administrator→IT Director, IT Service Manager→IT Director, IT Support Specialist→IT Service Manager) stay genuinely inconclusive — real org charts consistently show an intermediate title between these levels ("IT Support Manager," "Senior Systems Administrator") that this 15-role catalog doesn't include, a known simplification rather than a wrong edge. A People-side subject matter expert review is still the one open piece.

Role Transition Profiles

14 of the 15 roles have a completed Role Transition Profile — a role-specific companion to the Process View's Value Stream Profiles, showing what the framework's HITL maturity data means for that specific job. The remaining role (SVP Technology) currently computes to zero Responsible/Accountable touches under the framework's strict RACI rule; its transition story is shaped by span of control rather than process pattern-mix, which this doc type's current methodology can't yet produce. It stays held until the domain's reportsTo structure is confirmed — the same treatment applied to PCF 7.0's three zero-touch roles.
