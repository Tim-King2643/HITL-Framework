Organization Assessment Output

What the work-side assessment produces for an organization, and the hand-off that feeds the human-impact side
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress
What this is

The set of outputs an organization receives from its work-side assessment — an executive summary, a dashboard, the exceptions report and the process detail — and a defined hand-off of work-side results to the human-impact side. It covers the architecture of work only. Readiness and human impact are GiGi’s design; this spec produces what they build on and stops where they begin.

1. The problem

The sandbox can now assess an organization activity by activity, but what the organization gets back is a working tool, not an answer. An executive needs to see in a few minutes where the organization stands against the reference and what needs a decision. And the human-impact side needs a clean, defined set of work-side results to start from, rather than reading them off the sandbox.

2. Where the work side stops

GiGi’s Human Impact Pathway gives the seam:

Work Change → Role Impact → Human Impact → Manager Absorption → Team Effect → Accountability → Org Outcome

The work side produces the first two stages — what changed in the work, and which roles it lands on — plus the accountability facts it already holds (who is Accountable for each activity, and that accountability does not move with the work). Everything from Human Impact onward is the human-impact side. In GiGi’s executive view, this is “Story 1: Structural AI Opportunity”; “Story 2: Human Consequence” is hers.

4. The role hand-off

For each role, the work-side facts the human-impact side starts from. Every item is computed from assessment records and the reference; none is a judgment about people. The hand-off keeps two layers apart, so modeled potential is never read as an observed role change (GiGi, Oct 9, 2026):

  • Has moved (Assessed). What the organization’s assessed Current State shows today. Only assessed activities count here; an activity not yet assessed is reported as not assessed, never as unchanged.
  • Could move (Modeled). How far each activity may go, up to its Progression Ceiling or an approved override. This is potential, labeled Modeled everywhere it appears.
ItemWhat it carries
Work that has movedThe role’s assessed activities whose Current State is above Human-only, each with its Working Relationship category, evidence type and confidence. Assessed only.
Work that could moveEach activity’s Progression Ceiling (or approved override) and how many steps it sits from the Current State, with the activities where the role’s hands-on work would thin to exceptions or none (Agent-delegation, Automation). Modeled only.
Human judgment and review that remainsActivities where a person still does, decides or reviews every instance (Human-only, Judgment, Augmentation, Oversight), each with its Working Relationship category and Consequence of Error, shown for Current State and, separately, at the ceiling. With the work elements (GR-058), it also names which parts stay with the person. The work-side evidence for Judgment Density.
Role and accountability relationshipsFor each activity, whether the role is Accountable or Responsible, and the activities where it stays Accountable while the work moves to AI: the evidence for Accountability Concentration.
Consequence of Error and ceiling factorsEach activity’s Consequence of Error and the ceiling factors that hold its ceiling where it is.
Overrides and exceptionsCeiling overrides, proposal changes, past-the-ceiling findings and elements past their own ceiling on the role’s activities.
Manager and escalationThe role’s reporting line and where its High and Critical exceptions escalate (the Accountable role, GR-029), each marked validated or first-pass where the reporting line has not yet been confirmed.
BasisProvenance (Assessed or Modeled) on every figure, coverage (how many of the role’s activities are assessed), evidence type and confidence, and the reference version the hand-off was built from.

Delivered two ways: a role section in the executive summary and dashboard, and a downloadable data file (one row per role and activity, with the two layers in separate columns) the human-impact side can use directly.

5. Rules for every output

  • Every figure says whether it is Assessed or Modeled, and states coverage. An executive summary built on three assessed processes says so on its first line.
  • Roles, never names. No individual appears in any output.
  • Test data (ABC Test Org) carries its TEST label everywhere and is never cited as a finding.
  • The work side reports what changes in the work. It does not score readiness, predict workforce outcomes, or imply headcount.

6. Open questions

What the hand-off must carry — GiGi

Whether the items in section 4 are the ones the human-impact side needs, and at what level (role, process group, or both).

One report or two stories side by side — Tim and GiGi

Whether the executive summary eventually carries both stories in one document, or each side produces its own and they are read together.
