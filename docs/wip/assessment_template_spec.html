1. The problem

An assessment is only useful if two assessors looking at the same activity reach the same answer. That needs questions drawn from the locked category definitions, and a record of the evidence behind each answer, rather than a free choice from six labels.

2. The design

With an organization selected, each activity card has an Assess button (Reassess once assessed; View for anyone not signed in as Tim or GiGi). The panel shows what the reference knows — roles, Consequence of Error, the ceiling and its factors — then asks:

  1. Does AI play any part in this activity’s work? No → Human-only.
  2. Does AI only supply inputs (data, rankings, benchmarks) for a person’s own reasoning? Yes → Judgment.
  3. Does a person remain the primary actor, with AI assisting in real time and no separate approval step? Yes → Augmentation.
  4. AI produces the complete work: does a person review and approve every instance? Yes → Oversight.
  5. When the AI flags an exception, does that instance wait for a person to resolve it before it completes? Yes → Agent-delegation; no → Automation. After-the-fact monitoring, sampling or audit reports don’t count — that’s Automation.
RecordedRule
Working relationshipThe proposal, confirmed or changed; a change needs a reason.
EvidenceObserved practice, system configuration, audit or control test, interview or document — required.
Source roleThe role that provided or confirmed the answer — the activity’s Accountable or Responsible roles, defaulting to the Accountable role, or another role (required). Roles, not people: no individual’s name is recorded.
ReferenceFor system configuration, document, or audit or control test evidence: which system, document or audit. For system evidence the field names the activity’s systems of record from the work-product (CRUD) data — the activity’s own where confirmed, otherwise its process’s.
Confidence, noteHigh, medium or low (required); a free note.
Reference modelStamped on save (Sept 30): the reference version and this activity’s ceiling, Consequence of Error and ceiling factors at that moment. If any of them later change, the assessment is flagged “reference changed” for reassessment; its Current State is not changed.
HistoryEvery earlier version is kept. “Reset to baseline” returns the activity to Human-only.

On the ramp. At or below the ceiling, a step is filled with its color once the Current State reaches it, grey until then. Above the ceiling every step stays grey: a stage-colored ring marks a step inside an approved override (dashed until reached), a red ring a step reached with no override. The lock stays after the reference ceiling.

Test data. ABC Test Org carries a TEST chip and banner, is excluded from any comparison, and is never cited as a finding. It is saved through the Worker like any organization, so the test exercises the real path.

3. What is built

  • The panel, the questions, the record and its history, in the sandbox, saved through the Worker.
  • The ramp display, and the organization’s figures: assessed count, at ceiling, and past the ceiling with no override.
  • ASSESSED source chips (Sept 29): each assessed activity shows its date and source role; process and role rollups show ASSESSED, Assessed n/m or BASELINE, which is how progress shows by process and by role.
