Page 2 — Human Criticality Review

GiGi's six questions underneath the existing Low/Moderate/High/Critical scale — not a replacement for it, a foundation under it. Cited by name in Page 3's worked examples.

For a given activity, walk all six before naming a label. The label should trace back to specific answers, not be asserted first and rationalized after.

I would not replace Low / Moderate / High / Critical yet. I would first make sure we are asking the right questions underneath those labels.— GiGi Garcia, HITL Working Thoughts & Next Steps
QuestionWhat it's asking
1. Human impactWho could be harmed, and how?
2. Legal / regulatory exposureIs there a named statute or litigation category this touches?
3. Financial / operational impactWhat's the direct cost of a mistake — immediate or slow-building?
4. Trust / reputationCould a mistake damage trust — internal or external?
5. ReachOne person, a team, a workforce, or the enterprise?
6. ReversibilityHow hard is the mistake to undo once it happens?
LowNarrow reach, no legal exposure, easily reversible.
ModerateContained, correctable, limited exposure.
HighReal legal/regulatory exposure or serious individual impact, hard but not impossible to reverse.
CriticalSevere and/or wide-reach, direct legal exposure, very hard or impossible to undo.

Criticality and readiness (Page 1) are separate questions, checked independently — an activity can be high-criticality with strong readiness, or low-criticality with weak readiness. Page 3 walks both, plus the working-relationship and governance rules, against real activities.
