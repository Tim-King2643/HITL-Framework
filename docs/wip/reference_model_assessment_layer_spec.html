1. Why This Layer Exists

The framework describes no specific organization, so it cannot know how any activity is done today. Its Current State values were modeled judgments of typical practice, shown as if they were readings.

The same question had already come up for systems of record, which were changed from vendors to generic categories on Sept 26 (CL-008). Applied to Current State, it leads somewhere bigger. Tim’s answer:

“I would like to design the framework for exactly that purpose. To have an organization assessment layer. That would enable the framework to become a real working reference model that guides organizational assessments. I would initialize our current reference to the Human-only stage. The ceiling is our reference point for what needs to stay human by design.”

What the framework can say generically is what is true of the work itself: its consequence of error, the legal, relational and accountability limits on it, and so how far AI may take it. That is the Progression Ceiling. Where a particular organization stands today is a fact about that organization, and belongs to its assessment.

Progression CeilingThe furthest along the ramp AI may take the activity, set in the reference from its ceiling factors and Consequence of Error. Whatever lies above it stays human unless an override is approved.
Organization assessmentOne organization’s record of where each activity actually stands, with evidence, who gave it, and when.
Assessed Current StateAn activity’s position on the ramp in that organization. Exists only in an assessment.
AI design spaceThe distance from the assessed Current State to the ceiling: how much further the organization may take AI in that activity.
Past the ceilingAn assessed Current State above the ceiling with no override covering it: the organization already relies on AI more than the reference model permits. A finding to resolve, not an error in the assessment.
Ceiling overrideA governance decision raising one activity’s ceiling for one organization, kept apart from its assessment. Allowed only where Consequence of Error is Low or Moderate, and only on evidence that a named ceiling factor no longer holds (section 8b).
Governance ceilingAn organization’s own choice to hold an activity below the reference ceiling.

4. Granularity

  • Activity level for every assessment, because the ceiling and consequence live there.
  • Process and role level computed from activities, the same way the People View rolls up today, with coverage stated (“23 of 118 activities assessed”).
  • Organization level for the whole picture: design space remaining, activities past the ceiling with no override, and verification load across roles.

5. Decision Logic

Default view. The sandbox opens on the Reference Model with the Working Relationship view (Tim, Sept 26).

Resolution rule. For each activity: Current State is the organization’s assessed value where one exists, otherwise the Human-only baseline. The effective ceiling is the reference ceiling, the organization’s governance ceiling where it is lower, or its approved override where it is higher.

Ceilings can be lowered, not quietly raised. An organization may hold an activity lower as a governance choice. Raising it takes a ceiling override under the rule in section 8b, and the activity stays marked as a divergence from the reference model.

  • Work product and the Shock Wave: verification load becomes an organization finding, with an at-ceiling version for the reference.
  • Documentation (GR-048) describes Current State as an organization’s assessed value, and the ceiling as the reference limit an organization can raise only by override.
  • How the framework can be offered: the reference model as a product in its own right, assessments against it as engagements, and, with each organization’s consent, comparisons across assessed organizations. 8. Prototype and Pilot

Prototype with a test organization. Components cannot be designed or tested without organization data, so the layer is built against ABC Test Org: a test organization defaulted to the reference, with every activity at Human-only and every ceiling the reference ceiling. Its Current State is then set through the interactive assessment template, working-relationship assessment first, with other areas (governance ceilings, role mapping, CRUD) added as we determine.

Rules for test data. ABC Test Org carries a TEST chip on every value and a banner whenever it is selected; it is excluded from any benchmark or cross-organization comparison and is never cited as a finding. It is created through “New organization…” with the test box ticked and saved through the Worker like any organization, so the prototype exercises the real path.

Pressure test. Tim and GiGi assess ABC process by process in the Working Relationship view, starting with 7.2.1 Manage employee requisitions, 7.3.2 Manage employee performance and 7.6.2 Manage separation (15 activities across different parts of the ramp), and record where the questions, definitions or evidence prompts fail: ambiguous answers, boundary cases between categories, and time taken per activity. The template and the process are revised from that before any real organization is assessed.

8a. The Interactive Activity Assessment Template

Prototype built Sept 26, 2026, inside the sandbox’s Working Relationship view: choose an organization, open a process, and choose Assess on an activity.

For each activity the panel shows what the reference model knows (process, activity, Accountable and Responsible roles, Consequence of Error, and the ceiling with its factors), then asks a short sequence of questions drawn from the locked category definitions:

  1. Does AI play any part in this activity’s work? No → Human-only.
  2. Does AI only supply inputs (data, rankings, benchmarks) for a person’s own reasoning? Yes → Judgment.
  3. Does a person remain the primary actor, with AI assisting in real time and no separate approval step? Yes → Augmentation.
  4. AI produces the complete work: does a person review and approve every instance before it takes effect? Yes → Oversight.
  5. Does a person handle only the exceptions the AI flags? Yes → Agent-delegation. No person in the loop, exceptions included → Automation.

The answers propose a working relationship, which the assessor confirms or changes with a reason. The template then records the evidence type (observed practice, system configuration, interview, document), who gave it, a confidence level and a note, and flags the activity if the result is past its ceiling. Progress shows by process and by role.

On the ramp (Tim, Sept 26–27): at or below the ceiling, a step is filled with its Working-Relationship color once the Current State reaches it, and grey until then; the Reference Model shows only Human-only filled. Above the ceiling every step stays grey: a stage-colored ring marks a step inside an approved override (dashed until reached), and a red ring a step reached with no override. The lock stays right after the reference ceiling. The Assess button is white, like the view buttons, so it does not compete with the ramp.

8b. Ceiling Overrides

Tim, Sept 27, 2026 (GR-054): “It’s the ceiling factors [that] determine; the CoE should not be allowed to exceed Moderate.” Prototype built the same day, in the assessment panel.

An override raises one activity’s Progression Ceiling for one organization. It is a governance decision, not an assessment finding, and is stored apart from the assessment, with its own history.

ConditionRule
Consequence of ErrorLow or Moderate only. Where it is High or Critical the ceiling cannot be overridden, because the cost of an error does not change from one organization to the next.
Ceiling factorThe override names which of the activity’s own ceiling factors no longer holds in this organization (for example a technical readiness gap that has closed).
EvidenceWhat shows that the factor no longer holds.
ApprovalThe activity’s Accountable role, with the approver’s name.
Review dateRequired, one year by default, so an override does not quietly become permanent.

On the reference model, 49 of the 118 activities are eligible: Consequence of Error Low or Moderate, with a ceiling below Automation. An activity assessed past its ceiling with no override stays a finding, shown in red and counted in the organization’s figures, until it is brought back within the ceiling or an override is approved.

Where the rule is checked. The Worker checks every override against the reference itself, not against what the page sends. It reads each activity’s Consequence of Error, ceiling and ceiling factors from docs/sandbox/reference-wr.json, which a script generates from the sandbox’s master data and CI keeps in step with it. Each override records the version of the reference it was approved against.

8c. Proposed Revision: The Ceiling Factors Decide

For Tim and GiGi to decide (Tim, Sept 27, 2026: “It’s the ceiling factors [that] determine”). Nothing here is built yet; the Worker keeps the rule in section 8b until this is settled.

The problem. An organization cannot override a High Consequence of Error ceiling, but the reference itself gives 11 High activities a ceiling at Agent-delegation or Automation, where no person handles each instance. So the reference allows more than any organization could be approved for. The same rule should govern both.

Patterns were considered and set aside. Every one of the 11 that carries a pattern is Transaction, so a pattern cap cannot tell the ones that should come down from the ones that should stay; 9 Knowledge and Document activities already have Automation ceilings; 11 activities have no pattern; and patterns are retired from display and from the documentation (GR-048). The ceiling factors do separate the cases.

Ceiling factorIn the reference, where Consequence of Error is High or CriticalOverride
Governance / sign-offA person stays on every instance: the ceiling is at most Augmentation.Allowed at Low or Moderate, where the sign-off is the organization’s own choice. Not at High or Critical.
Legal / fiduciaryAbove Augmentation only when the ceiling names the control that catches errors at scale (reconciliation, audit, exception reporting).Allowed at Low or Moderate, naming the control. Not at High or Critical.
Technical readiness gapNo constraint.Allowed at any Consequence of Error, because the gap can close. The only case where a High ceiling can be overridden.
Human judgment floor, accountability, relationalNo constraint beyond the ceiling itself.Never. These describe the work, not a gap an organization can close.

Consequence of Error still sets the bar: at High or Critical, only a technical readiness gap can be named in an override.

Effect on overrides. Of the 49 activities eligible today (Low or Moderate, ceiling below Automation), 15 would no longer be eligible, because their only factors are the human judgment floor or accountability. They include 7.2.1.1 and 7.3.2.1, the two Moderate activities in the pressure-test processes. 7.3.4.5 would become eligible through its technical factor.

When decided. Ceilings that change are logged in the changelog; each control is recorded on its activity in the master data; the rule is added to the Worker’s check, which already reads each activity’s factors from the reference lookup.

9. Known Open Constructs

Ceilings under review

Whether the 42 Automation ceilings, and the rest, hold up now that they carry the reference model’s central claim.

Raising a ceiling

The rule is set (section 8b), with a proposed revision in section 8c for decision. Still open: what happens when an override’s review date passes, and whether a Moderate override needs a second approver.

Organization roles

Real organizations have their own roles. Mapping them to the reference role taxonomy is needed before role-level findings mean anything.

Reference versions

When a ceiling changes after an organization is assessed, which version its findings are read against. Overrides already record the reference version they were approved against; assessments do not yet.

Evidence standard and confidentiality

What evidence an assessment requires, and how organization data is protected and, if ever, aggregated.

10. Sourcing, Validation & Scope Boundary

Source is Tim’s design decision of Sept 26, 2026 and Sandbox 7.0’s current data (118 classified activities; ceilings: 28 Judgment, 5 Oversight, 8 Augmentation, 35 Agent-delegation, 42 Automation). Scope is PCF 7.0 in the sandbox; the design is domain-neutral so every domain can use it. Production is out of scope until the full production rebuild.
