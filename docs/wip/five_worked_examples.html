Five Worked Examples — The Working-Relationship Pressure Test

GiGi's own proposed test, run against the live PCF 7.0 data and RACI: her five chosen activities, walked through her own chain — activity → working relationship → consequence/criticality → human readiness → governance limit → transition recommendation.
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress

In her working notes, GiGi proposed we stop adding theory and instead run a small set of very different activities all the way through the same logic — not to prove the methodology, but to break it, find redundancy, and expose blind spots. She named five activities and a fixed chain to walk each one through: current activity → proposed working relationship → consequence/criticality → human readiness → governance limit → transition recommendation. This runs that test against the framework's real, live data — the fully classified PCF 7.0 dataset (118 of 118 L4 activities) and the actual RACI and role-taxonomy tables — rather than illustrative examples.

All five of her named activities exist in the live data under their exact or near-exact wording. Codes, classifications, and RACI below are pulled directly from the live sandbox and role taxonomy, not reconstructed for this page.

The goal of the prototype should not be to prove our methodology. It should be to break it, identify redundancy, expose blind spots, and determine what actually produces an executive-grade insight.

— GiGi Garcia, HITL Working Thoughts & Next Steps

A Note on Method

One thing became clear before walking through any of the five individually: all five of GiGi's chosen activities already sit at their Progression Ceiling in the live model. Their Current State, Future State, and Progression Ceiling values are identical — the framework has already independently concluded, activity by activity, that none of these should move further toward automation regardless of what the technology can do. That collapses the first two steps of her chain into one for every example below (there is no daylight between "current" and "proposed" to walk through), which is itself a finding worth stating plainly rather than glossing over: the working-relationship and consequence logic is doing real work here, correctly holding the line on exactly the kind of activity she picked these five to stress-test.

Readiness is a different story, and where this test earns its keep. There is no scored Human Readiness Gate wired into the framework yet — that instrument is GiGi's own open canvas to design, not something to fake a number for here. So the Human Readiness read below is a first-pass qualitative one, grounded in what the live data can actually tell us today: who is Accountable and Responsible per the RACI, whether that role's own reporting line has been confirmed or is still provisional per the taxonomy's self-consistency check, and whether the role doing the real judgment work sits inside the 14-role HR catalog at all or outside it as an "External Participant." That is a narrower question than her full eight-dimension model, but it is real, sourced, and — as it turns out — sharp enough to surface concrete gaps.

Governance Limit draws on her own proposed structure for a Governed Decision Point — a named owner, an escalation path, an override path, documentation, and review — applied to what the consequence-of-error tag and RACI already tell us, rather than inventing new machinery.

Hold at Judgment — correct as-is. But this is the clearest example of a readiness blind spot in the set: the next readiness work needs a way to reach past the HR role catalog to hiring managers themselves, or the "who is ready to exercise this judgment" question has no real answer.

Hold at Judgment. Treat HRBP Manager's reportsTo confirmation as a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have, before calling this ceiling durable — and reconsider upgrading Legal from Informed to Consulted given what the activity's own risk note already says.

6Where the Method Held, Where It Broke

On the working-relationship side, the method held up well. All five of GiGi's deliberately hard cases — picked specifically to stress different parts of the model — independently landed at Judgment, or Augmentation with a hard ceiling. That's the right answer for EEOC exposure, NLRB exposure, an executive accountability decision, litigation-grade documentation, and a bias-sensitive succession call. The consequence-of-error tagging and the Progression Ceiling logic aren't decorative here; they're doing real work, and doing it consistently.

Readiness is where it breaks, and in a genuinely useful way rather than a vague one. Three of the five run through one of the exact two reportsTo edges the role taxonomy already flags as unconfirmed — HRBP Manager or Director of L&D. Two of the five have their real decision-maker (Hiring Managers, line Managers) sitting entirely outside the 14-role catalog as an External Participant, meaning the framework's own readiness and reporting apparatus doesn't reach the person actually exercising the judgment. And one RACI inconsistency turned up independent of readiness — Legal downgraded to Informed on the one activity (grievances) where its own risk note argues hardest for Consulted.

None of this is hypothetical. It's the same gap GiGi named in her own working notes, now pinned to five specific, real activities instead of argued in the abstract — which was the whole point of running the test this way.

Just because AI can perform the work does not mean the organization is ready — or should permit it — to do so.
