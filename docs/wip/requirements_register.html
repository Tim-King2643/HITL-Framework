How this register works
ProgressMeans
OpenRaised and recorded. No design yet.
DesignedA design is drafted in a spec or WIP item.
In progressPart of it is built; the next action names what is missing.
BuiltBuilt and meeting its acceptance criteria, waiting for both of us to accept it.
DoneBuilt and accepted by both Tim and GiGi.
ClosedNo further work: superseded or resolved another way.
  • Progress and decisions are separate. Progress says how far the work has got. A decision is something Tim and GiGi are asked to settle: accept built work, approve a design, or answer a question. Each requirement has at most one pending decision.
  • How a decision is made. Each of us chooses Approve, Not approve or Defer, with an optional note. It is decided when both choose the same; if we differ it shows as Split until we talk it through. Either of us can change a choice.
  • One place per decision. Where a decision is taken through a WIP item’s vote, the card links there instead of showing its own buttons.
  • Recording. Choices save immediately. Claude folds settled decisions into the register’s file, moves the progress on (Built to Done once accepted), and sets the next decision.
  • Evidence. Progress moves on only when the acceptance criteria are met, with the evidence in the requirement’s history.
  • Carried over (Sept 28, 2026). Requirements marked Implemented before this change were built but never formally accepted by both of us; they are now Built, each with an Accept decision.

Standard Design Spec Template — the shape every design spec follows (revised Sept 28, 2026)
SectionPurposeContent contract
Title blockIdentify the document.Title, one-line subtitle, the requirement(s) it designs, authors and date.
Decision briefLet a reader stop after one screen.About 250 words. What this is (two sentences). Decided (bullets, each dated). Awaiting your decision (the decision block, read from this register, with its buttons). If approved (what changes). Nothing else.
1. The problemWhy the work is needed.A short paragraph with the real evidence. No history of how the idea developed.
2. The designThe current design only.The model, data and rules as they stand now, precise enough to apply. Superseded versions are not described.
3. What is builtWhere it can be seen.What exists, with links, and what does not yet.
4. Open questionsKeep honesty structural.One line each: the question and who decides it. A question with a proposal to decide becomes the requirement’s pending decision instead.
Reference (folded)Detail on demand.Long tables, worked examples, sourcing and scope — closed by default.
  • Length: about 250 words for the brief and 1,500 for the body. When a spec grows past that, split it, one spec per requirement.
  • History lives in the register: dated notes, quotes and build records go in the requirement’s log here, not in the spec.
  • Decisions live in the register: the spec shows the same decision block, so a choice made on either page shows on both.
