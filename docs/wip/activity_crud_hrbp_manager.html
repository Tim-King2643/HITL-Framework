1. How the Draft Was Built

  • Reasoned from the activity. For each activity, the question is what that activity itself creates, reads, updates and deletes. Nothing is inherited from the process.
  • Cross-checked against the old process CRUD. The activity links are rolled up to the process and compared. Links the old CRUD never had are marked New; old links no activity supports are marked Not supported. These are the review list.
  • An update implies a read. An activity that updates a work product is not also listed as reading it.
  • Catalog only. Every link names a Work-Product Catalog entry. Outputs the catalog lacks are listed in section 5 rather than invented.
  • Hover a link to see the reasoning where it is not obvious.

3. What Changes for the HRBP Manager

The Work Product spec's verification-point rule, run on this draft: an activity that reads or updates a work product relies on it, and that is a verification point when another producer of the work product is at Augmentation or further along the ramp, and the relying activity is at Judgment or Oversight or carries a High or Critical Consequence of Error. Producers outside these eleven processes are still judged at process level.

At process level the same rule gives the HRBP Manager 7 work products to verify; at activity level it gives 9. Activity level sees checks inside a process, which process level cannot: the approval in 7.2.1.3 checks the requisition that 7.2.1.2 opened automatically, and offboarding in 7.6.2.3 relies on a Separation Record that retirement also updates, automatically. The Workforce Plan comes in through 7.2.1.1 and 7.2.1.3. The ER Case Record drops out, because nothing automated produces it any more (Q1). The list also names which activity carries each check.

4. Tim’s Decisions — Sept 26, 2026

Q1 — 7.6.2’s update of the ER Case Record: dropped

None of 7.6.2’s four activities changes an employee-relations case. The ER Case Record is now produced only in 7.4.1, at Judgment, so it is no longer AI-touched.

Q2 — The 10 new links: all accepted

Marked New under each process above, together with the two links to the work products added under Q5.

Q3 — Separation Record: offboarding creates it

7.6.2.3 Manage offboarding creates it; 7.6.2.1 Manage retirement updates it.

Q4 — “An update implies a read”: kept

An activity that updates a work product is not also listed as reading it.

Q5 — Outputs the catalog lacks: added when identified

Two added now: WP-7-38 Staffing Plan, created in 7.2.1.1, and WP-7-39 Performance Program, updated in 7.3.2.4 (its creator is not identified yet).

5. What the Review Surfaced

Reasoning from the activity catches process-level errors. It found two: the requisition, created in 7.2.1.2 rather than in 7.1.2 (Tim’s catch, CL-006), and 7.6.2’s update of the ER Case Record (dropped, Q1). Both came from the old process CRUD.

The Performance Review’s AI involvement comes from outside the HRBP’s process. Inside 7.3.2, the most automated producer is 7.3.2.3 Document employee performance, at Augmentation; 7.3.2.4, at Agent-delegation, monitors the program rather than producing reviews. The review is still AI-touched at Agent-delegation, through 7.3.3.3 Manage employee skill and competency development in the Director of L&D’s process, which is still at process level.

Six of the eleven processes had CRUD not yet confirmed (7.4.2, 7.4.3, 7.4.4, 7.6.1, 7.6.3, 7.8.1). Their CRUD is now derived from confirmed activity links.

6. Next Step

The next roles to run the same way are the HRIS Analyst and the HR Generalist. Each finished process switches to derived CRUD, one process at a time; the other processes keep their stored CRUD until then, shown in grey on each activity in the Data View.
