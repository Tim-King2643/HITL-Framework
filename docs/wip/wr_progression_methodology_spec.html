Working-Relationship Progression

What the method does and how it is used — a plain-language companion to the full methodology

What this is

The method under the whole sandbox: every activity is placed on a six-step ramp by what is actually happening in the work, with a Progression Ceiling saying how far AI may take it and why. A plain-language companion to the full methodology.

1. The problem

The old model predicted how much of an activity needed a human from its type — Decision, Knowledge, Document, Transaction or Exception. Activities of different types kept landing on the same real answer (a person checks every instance), while activities with nearly the same score involved entirely different work. The type was a poor stand-in for what matters: what the person is doing, and why it has to be a person.

2. The design

One ramp. Human-only → Judgment → Oversight → Augmentation → Agent-delegation → Automation. An activity moves one step at a time.

Two values per activity. Current State, where it stands — Human-only in the reference, and assessed for each organization. Progression Ceiling, the furthest AI may take it, with the reasons (its ceiling factors) and its Consequence of Error.

Detail where the evidence is. Classification lives at the activity. A role, a manager or a team is measured as a rollup of what changes beneath it.

Three checks before moving on. No activity advances on one score: evidence that the technology performs on this work, that the process around it can support the change, and that the people affected are ready.

One question set for the ripple. As a change moves out from the activity to the role, the person, the manager, the team and whoever is accountable, the same six questions are asked: what changed, who absorbs it, where did the work go, what new risk appeared, what capability is needed, and who owns the outcome now.

3. What is built

  • All 118 PCF 7.0 activities in the sandbox, with ceilings, ceiling factors, Consequence of Error and the step-by-step ramp.
  • Walked by hand for three pilot roles spanning the range: HRBP Manager (where it surfaced a reportsTo correction), Recruiter (highest volume) and CHRO (narrowest scope). The method held across all three without change.
  • Not yet: instruments for the three checks (GR-012, GR-037), and the ripple as a sandbox feature (GR-033).

4. Open questions

Effort, not just share

The model says how much stays human, not whether what remains is easy or demanding (GR-018).

Meaningful control

Whether people have the authority, information and safety to challenge a wrong AI recommendation (GR-013).

Roles without formal decision rights

Relationship and coaching work that the accountability record cannot see (GR-031).

Technology-readiness evidence

What it should consist of, at scale (GR-037).

Reference: source and the CHRO edge cases

Grounded against the APQC Process Classification Framework source file, obtained from APQC. The reporting-line review is a separate, parallel track whose findings feed how the method measures a manager’s capacity to absorb change. Two edge cases from the CHRO pilot remain: what the comparison group is for a role with no peer, and who absorbs a change when there is no manager above. Full detail: the methodology.
