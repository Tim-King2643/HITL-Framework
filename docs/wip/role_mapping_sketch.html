Their titles, everywhere our roles appear

Approach

Option 1: a separate role map. Built outside the app (their title list, a pre-map, validation by walking the activities with their HR lead, sign-off), then loaded in. The role catalog, RACI and records stay shared and keyed to the catalog roles. Each organization saves one more record: a role map from its own job titles to the catalog roles, with the match type and who confirmed it. When the organization is selected, every place a catalog role appears shows their title instead; hover any name for the catalog role behind it.

The titles here are made up for ABC Test Org to show every match type. The activities, RACI and decision point records are read live from the sandbox.

Show roles as
People view, group by

Option 2: map while walking the activities. The validation step, done in the app. For each activity, the organization answers “who here actually holds this?” by picking one of its own titles for each catalog role in the RACI. Two things come out of it:

  • the role map builds itself from the picks, match types included;
  • where their answer differs from the catalog, it is kept as that activity’s own RACI for the organization, and listed as a difference: a finding, not drift.

The catalog stays shared and untouched. Picks start from Option 1’s titles so you can see the result; change any of them. One pick on 7.2.3.4 is set to show a difference. Nothing here is saved.

Process
Add one of their titles
The display rules
  • One-to-one their title replaces ours.
  • Many-to-one all their titles are listed.
  • Split one title of theirs covers two catalog roles; it shows in both places, marked with the role it is acting as.
  • Unknown no title maps to a catalog role: shown in amber as “Unknown (role)”. On High or Critical work it holds the Readiness Gate until resolved.
  • Theirs only a title of theirs the catalog does not have: listed in the map, outside the model.
Recommended

Option 2, with Option 1’s worksheet as its starting point: the activity walk is where an organization says how its work really happens, which is what the mapping has to capture, and it fits GiGi’s GR-022 reality test. Readiness stays recorded per catalog role, even when one title covers two: a person can be ready for one role’s work and not the other’s. The People view can combine a split title’s work under one panel, since that is how their people experience it.
