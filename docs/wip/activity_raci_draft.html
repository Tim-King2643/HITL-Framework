Activity-Level RACI

Who is Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed for each of the 118 activities, reasoned from the activity rather than inherited from its process
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress
Draft for Tim’s review — one decision: apply it as drafted (GR-060)

RACI is the last major component still set per process: every activity inherits its process’s four letters. CRUD moved to the activity level on Oct 2 (CL-014) and the work-element model works per activity (GR-058). This draft does the same for RACI, built the way the CRUD drafts were. The recommendations are built in; section 4 says what applying it changes.

1. How the Draft Was Built

  • Reasoned from the activity. Each activity starts from its process’s RACI. A letter changes only where the activity’s own work gives a reason, and the reason is written beside it.
  • One Accountable per activity. The role that answers for the activity’s outcome.
  • Responsible is always named. Where the Accountable role does the work itself, it is listed as Responsible too. The “from Accountable” fallback is retired.
  • Executive Accountable where an activity sets direction for the function or a major part of it. The CHRO for HR strategy and total rewards strategy; SVP Talent Management for talent strategy (succession, leadership programs, and the design of the talent acquisition and development programs); VP People & Culture for culture, the employee experience, DEI and engagement. This widens the July 27 rule, which reserved Executive Accountable for 7.1.1 alone. At the process level that rule fit; at the activity level it would leave direction-setting work with directors and two executives with no accountability at all.
  • Directors own the function’s programs and policies; individual contributors are Accountable only for transactional work within their own job (posting a requisition, enrolling benefits, maintaining applicant data).
  • A High or Critical decision sits with a role senior enough to answer for it. Exceptions at those levels go to the Accountable role (the Consequence of Error rule), so the Accountable role has to be one that can take them.
  • Consulted and Informed only where the activity gives a reason. Letters the process carried that an activity has no use for are dropped from that activity.
  • Single-activity processes keep their process RACI, with Responsible named. Moving to the activity level adds nothing where the process is the activity. Process-level questions about them (such as whether VP People & Culture should own the communication plan, 7.8.1) are out of scope here.
  • External participants stay marked with an asterisk (Hiring Managers*, Managers*, Legal*, Finance*, and Marketing*, new on 7.1.1.9). They are never fitted into a catalog role.

    4. Applying It

    Recommended, as one change once approved:

    • Master data. The four letters move into the sandbox as activity-level master data (ACTIVITY_RACI), beside ACTIVITY_CRUD. Process RACI is no longer stored; it is derived from the activities.
    • A process’s Accountable role, derived. The role Accountable for most of its activities, with the others shown as “also Accountable for” and a count. A process RACI view still reads one owner per process, without hiding the activities that sit elsewhere.
    • Role rollups from activities. A role’s % H and its People view rows come from the activities it is Accountable for, not from whole processes.
    • One change-log entry (kind raci, a decision), and the CI audit extended to track ACTIVITY_RACI so a later RACI edit cannot go in without one.
    • ABC Test Org. Assessments and overrides record the Accountable role. Where the role changes on an assessed activity, the assessment is flagged to re-confirm its role, the same way a reference change is flagged today. Section 2 lists them.
