Ceiling Overrides

When an organization may raise an activity’s Progression Ceiling, and how the override is kept honest
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress
What this is

A ceiling override raises one activity’s Progression Ceiling for one organization. It is a governance decision, signed off by the activity’s process owner and kept apart from the assessment; an activity assessed past its ceiling with no override stays a finding.

Decided
  • Sept 27: allowed only where Consequence of Error is Low or Moderate, naming one of the activity’s own ceiling factors that no longer holds, with evidence and the Accountable role’s approval.
  • Sept 27: the Worker checks every override against the reference itself, not against what the page sends.
  • Sept 28: no fixed review date and no second approver. The process owner’s audits are recorded as reviews; an override is flagged when the activity’s reference values change.
  • Sept 29: approvals and reviews record the Accountable role, not a person’s name.
Awaiting your decision
If approved

7.1.2.6, 7.2.3.3 and 7.4.5 come down to Oversight; the seven Automation activities each record their control; the rule goes into the Worker’s check. 15 activities whose only factors are the judgment floor or accountability can no longer be overridden, including 7.2.1.1 and 7.3.2.1.

Designs GR-054. Part of GR-052 reference model.

1. The problem

An organization cannot override a High Consequence of Error ceiling, yet the reference gives 11 High activities a ceiling at Agent-delegation or Automation, where no person handles each instance. The reference allows more than any organization could be approved for. One rule should govern both, and the ceiling factors — the reasons each ceiling sits where it does — are what separate the cases.

2. The design

The rule today

ConditionRule
Consequence of ErrorLow or Moderate only.
Ceiling factorNames which of the activity’s own ceiling factors no longer holds in this organization.
Evidence and approvalWhat shows the factor no longer holds; the Accountable role that approved it. Reviews record the Accountable role that confirmed it. No one’s name is recorded.
ReviewEach audit by the process owner is recorded (date, who confirmed, note); the panel shows how long ago. A review re-checks the override against the current reference and is refused if it no longer meets the rule.

The proposed revision: the ceiling factors decide

Ceiling factorIn the reference, at High or CriticalOverride
Governance / sign-offA person stays on every instance: the ceiling is at most Augmentation.At Low or Moderate only.
Legal / fiduciaryAbove Augmentation only when the ceiling names the control that catches errors at scale.At Low or Moderate only, naming the control.
Technical readiness gapNo constraint.At any Consequence of Error: the gap can close.

Patterns were considered as the criterion and set aside: all 11 High activities that carry a pattern are Transaction, so a pattern cap cannot separate them.

AI-touched inputs (added Oct 6, from the work-product analysis, GR-046): each work product the activity reads or updates whose most automated other producer is at Augmentation or further, at the Progression Ceiling. Each is shown with that producer, which is at Automation unless marked. For the seven kept at Automation, these are what the named control has to catch, since no person stands between them and the activity: payroll’s control must cover four inputs, offboarding’s six. The nine activities with inputs are exactly the nine where the Work Product view finds an unchecked hand-off; the two found separately agree. 7.4.5 and 7.3.4.5 have none.

7.3.4.5 is the odd one: its factors are legal and a technical readiness gap, not governance, so it would be the one High activity an organization could override.

3. What is built

  • The Ceiling override section of the assessment panel: record, change, remove, and record a review.
  • The Worker’s check against reference-wr.json, generated from the master data and kept in step by CI; each override records the reference version it was approved against.
  • The ramp rings, and the organization’s “past ceiling, no override” count.
  • Not yet: the proposed revision, pending the decision above.
