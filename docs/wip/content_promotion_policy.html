Content Promotion Policy

How a change earns its way from the sandbox to the live site, on the record

What this is

How a change earns its way from an idea to the live site. Three tiers — Sandbox, WIP and Production — a review ladder for content, and a lighter lane for master-data corrections, so nothing drifts between them unrecorded.

  • Sept 24: master-data corrections skip the review ladder: record the change in the changelog, edit the sandbox first, then publish to production. The CI check enforces the changelog entry.
  • Sept 28: decisions on requirements are made in the register; where a requirement is decided through a WIP item, its ladder vote is the decision.

The policy is the rule for every change: content climbs the ladder to Published, master data takes the lighter lane, and both are recorded in the changelog.

Designs GR-045. Related: GR-043 (the changelog and its CI check).

1. The problem

On Sept 21 the tagline turned out to exist in three wordings across the live site, the sandbox and the WIP catalog, with no record of which was ever deliberately shipped. Content moved between three informal spaces with nothing marking the boundaries, nothing requiring a deliberate act to cross them, and nothing recording that a crossing happened.

2. The design

TierForNot
SandboxFree exploration with Tim and GiGi, and the master copy of reasoned data (roles, working relationships, CRUD).Not a staging step: nothing flows out of it automatically.
WIPProposals under review and vote, and the living trackers.“Adopted” is a review outcome, not a publish event.
ProductionThe live site, from the repo.Nothing is production until it is committed and live.

The ladder for content: Proposed → Under Review → Concept Agreed → Text Approved → Adopted → Published. The key rule: Adopted is not Published. An item is done only when it is committed, verified live, and logged.

The lane for master data (a reportsTo edge, a ceiling, a CRUD link): no ladder and no vote, because the evidence is the review. 1. Record it in the changelog, with who asked, the evidence and how it was verified. 2. Edit the sandbox’s master copy. 3. Tim publishes the value to production. CI fails any push that changes tracked master data without a new changelog entry.

Before anything is marked Published: the committed version is the adopted one; every file holding the same value is updated together; the change is verified live, not assumed from the commit; and it is logged.

Who does what: Tim alone commits to production. Claude prepares each change and verifies it landed. Tim and GiGi both review in WIP.

3. What is built

  • The ladder on every votable WIP item, with two voting rounds (concept, then text) and a separate publish step.
  • The Production Changelog (CL-001 onward) and the CI check that enforces it.
  • The master-data lane, used for every correction since CL-001.

4. Open questions

The tagline — Tim and GiGi

Confirm its final wording and take it through the ladder to Published, the first item end to end.

Domain rebuilds — Tim

Apply the policy from day one in each domain’s production rebuild rather than retrofitting it.
