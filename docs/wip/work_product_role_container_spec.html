Work Product in the Role Container

How a role's work product changes as AI takes a larger part: what the role produces, who relies on it, and what the role must verify
Documentation Dashboard Work in Progress
Concept stage — design for GR-046, not yet built

Extends The Shock Wave Effect's “A Second Path: Work Product” into a buildable design. Every figure here is modeled from Sandbox 7.0 data, not observed. It covers the work side only: what happens to the work product inside a role. What the change means for the person holding the role is the human-transition side, and this spec does not substitute for it.

Related Requirements — Requirements Register
  • GR-046 — work product transition within the role container (the requirement this spec designs)
  • GR-029 — Exception Escalation Effect (verification load is its first measurable form)
  • GR-020, GR-024, GR-027 — role evolution, the composite executive view, per-role narratives

1. Why This Layer Exists

A role is the container; what changes inside it is the work product. The framework could already show which activities move along the working-relationship ramp. It could not show where the output of that work goes, or who has to check it.

GiGi named the cost the verification tax (HR Couch, Sept 23, 2026): AI-produced work does not remove effort, it moves it to whoever must confirm the output before acting on it. Tracing the Performance Review through Sandbox 7.0 makes it concrete. It is produced by 7.3.2 Manage employee performance, where one activity already runs at Agent-delegation and another at Augmentation, and it feeds promotion (7.6.1), pay and recognition (7.5.1), labor relations (7.4.1), and retention (7.5.3). The HRBP Manager is Accountable at the source and again at two of those four decisions, so pays the verification tax twice.

Activity-level classification alone does not see this. This layer measures where AI-produced work lands, and on whom.

2. Core Model

TermDefinition
Work productA named output of an activity that other activities use: a record, review, plan, or report.
Production modeHow the work product is produced, taken from the producing activity's working relationship: its Current State and Progression Ceiling.
ConsumerAn activity that reads the work product, with its Accountable role, working relationship, and Consequence of Error.
Verification pointA place where an AI-produced work product meets a human decision that relies on it, and someone must confirm where it came from, whether it was verified, and who stands behind the decision.
Verification loadThe number of AI-touched work products a role relies on at verification points. A count of exposure, not hours.
Pays twiceA role that is Accountable where a work product is produced and again where it is consumed.
Role containerFor one role, three views: what it produces, who relies on it, and what it must verify.
Redefinition thresholdThe point at which enough of a role's production reaches Agent-delegation or Automation that the role itself needs redefining.

3. Data Model

Two new structures, plus values derived from data the sandbox already holds.

StructureFieldsStatus
Work-product catalogID, name, one-line definition, system of recordNew
Activity–work-product linkActivity code, work-product ID, operation (create, read, update, delete)New; replaces process-level CRUD
Production modeProducing activity's Current State and Progression CeilingDerived from existing classification
Consumer profileReading activity's Accountable role, working relationship, and Consequence of ErrorDerived from existing RACI and classification

Today the work products exist only as free-text strings inside process-level CRUD entries, such as “Performance Review (Performance Management System).” Sandbox 7.0 carries 113 such entries naming 37 distinct work products across all 37 processes (39 before the Sept 25 catalog review merged two pairs).

4. Granularity

  • Activity level for every create, read, update, and delete link, because production mode comes from one activity's classification. In 18 of the 37 processes more than one activity shares the process's CRUD, so process level cannot say which activity produces a work product.
  • Work-product level for fan-out and verification points, because one work product can feed many processes (the Employee Master Record is read 14 times).
  • Role level for the container view and verification load, rolled up through RACI Accountable, the same way the People View already rolls up working relationships.

5. Decision Logic

Production-mode rule. A work product takes its production mode from the activity that produces it: that activity's Current State and Progression Ceiling. Only creates and updates produce; reads and deletes do not.

  • More than one producer. Each create or update link carries its own activity's mode. The work product as a whole shows the producer furthest along the ramp, and names it, because any AI contribution makes the output AI-touched for everyone downstream. Current State and Progression Ceiling are each taken this way. In Sandbox 7.0, 12 of the 37 work products are created or updated in more than one process, such as the Employee Master Record, which is created in 7.7.3 and updated in 7.6.1, 7.6.2 and 7.6.3.
  • Derived, never stored. Production mode is read from the activity's classification each time it is shown. Changing an activity's classification, logged once in the changelog, changes every work product it produces, with no second entry to keep in step.
  • Gaps shown as gaps. An unclassified producer, or an Open / undecided ceiling, is displayed as such. It is never read as human-only.

Verification point (proposed rule). A read is a verification point when the producing activity's Current State is Augmentation or further along the ramp, and the reading activity is at Judgment or Oversight or carries a High or Critical Consequence of Error. Uses existing data only.

Redefinition threshold (new, value not yet set). The share of a role's produced work products whose production mode reaches Agent-delegation or Automation. The sandbox already flags redefinition per step inside each activity; this rule lifts it to the role. The threshold value is for Tim and GiGi to set.

6. Instrumentation and Build Work

A. Enabling work — making work product real at the activity level

  1. Work-product catalog. Normalize the free-text names into catalog entries with ID, definition, and system of record. Master data in the sandbox since Sept 26; see the Work-Product Catalog. done, definitions to confirm
  2. Activity-level CRUD. Move each create, read, update, and delete from the process to the specific activity, starting with the 18 processes that hold more than one activity. Reasoned from each activity, not inherited from the process; a finished process’s CRUD is derived from its activities. First role done: HRBP Manager. in progress
  3. Confirm the CRUD data. All 37 processes carry CRUD entries, but the sandbox marks only 22 as confirmed. Confirm or correct the other 15.
  4. Production-mode rule. A work product takes its producing activity's Current State and Progression Ceiling. Written in section 5, including the multi-producer case. written
  5. Consumer links. For every read, attach the reading activity's Accountable role, working relationship, and Consequence of Error.
  6. Verification-point rule. Adopt the rule in section 5, or a revised one.
  7. Verification-load and pays-twice definitions. Decide whether to weight the count by Consequence of Error; label it as exposure, not hours.
  8. Redefinition threshold. Set the value.
  9. Master-data governance. Treat the catalog and activity-level CRUD as master data: edit the sandbox first, log each change in the changelog, and extend the CI check to cover them.
  10. Provenance. Every derived count carries the MODELED chip and info box used for % H.

B. UI work — work-product elements in the sandbox

  1. Role work-product panel in People View, expandable under each role: Produces, Relied on by, Must verify. buildable now
  2. Work-product chip showing production mode in the Rule 20 colors, as Current State → Progression Ceiling. needs A2
  3. Work products on each activity card: what the activity creates, updates, and reads. needs A2
  4. Work-product trace view: pick a work product and follow it from producer to every consumer, with verification points highlighted. needs A2
  5. Verification-load indicator per role, with the pays-twice marker and the MODELED chip. buildable now
  6. Role-level redefinition flag. needs A8
  7. Legend and info-box definitions for work product, production mode, verification point, and verification load.
  8. Data View upgrade: activity rows show their own CRUD instead of inheriting the process's. needs A2

“Buildable now” items work on today's process-level data, labeled as such, and gain precision once A2 lands.

7. Downstream Effects

The work product carries change outward along a second path next to the Shock Wave's five rings: activity → work product → consuming activity → its Accountable role → that role's manager and team. Verification load is the first computed signal for the Manager and Team rings, which the framework does not measure today.

9. Known Open Constructs

Effort, not just exposure

Verification load counts work products, not hours. Turning it into effort needs observed data the framework does not have.

Consequence weighting

Whether a review feeding a promotion should count more than one feeding a newsletter, and by how much.

Redefinition threshold value

The rule is defined; the number is not. It is a judgment for Tim and GiGi.

Unconfirmed CRUD

15 of the 37 processes carry CRUD entries not yet marked confirmed; results that depend on them are provisional.

10. Sourcing, Validation & Scope Boundary

Source data is Sandbox 7.0: the process structure, RACI, CRUD entries, and the working-relationship and Consequence-of-Error classifications. All derived figures are modeled. Scope is Sandbox 7.0; production is out of scope until the full production rebuild. The design is written to be domain-neutral so later domains can reuse it. It does not claim to measure effort, and it does not address what the change means for the person in the role.
