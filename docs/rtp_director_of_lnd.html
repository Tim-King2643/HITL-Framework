The single Decision-pattern activity — 7.3.3.4, Establish succession plans — carries the highest M1 baseline (92%) of anything in this role's scope and the shallowest Δ (-12 pts), meaning it's the most resistant to automation of everything Director of L&D owns. The Transaction-pattern cluster (DEI plan implementation, policy administration, workforce-model implementation, job-family management, training delivery, certifications) is where the real near-term automation exposure sits, not the Knowledge-heavy strategy work.

A Third Shape of Transition

HRBP Manager's profile was broad and multi-pattern; HRIS Analyst's was Transaction-dominant reinvention; CHRO's was orchestration from the top. Director of L&D is different again: a genuinely mixed portfolio where strategy-setting (Knowledge) and program delivery (Transaction) sit side by side in the same role, anchored by one high-stakes judgment call — succession planning — that stays human almost entirely.

Josh Bersin's February 2026 corporate-learning research is unusually well-matched to this shape. His four-level Learning Maturity Model — Static Training, Scaled Learning, Integrated Development, and Dynamic Enablement — describes almost exactly the same split this role's data shows: the Knowledge-heavy strategy and design work maps to where L&D leadership sets direction, while the Transaction-heavy delivery work maps to what his research shows is ripest for AI-native replacement.

Skills & Competencies

Bersin's research names a specific, measurable problem: 74% of companies report they aren't keeping up with their organization's demand for new skills, against a $400 billion corporate training market. His diagnosis is that the underlying operating model — not the content — is what's failed.

  • Dynamic-content system literacy — evaluating AI-native learning platforms that generate and connect content on demand, replacing static SCORM courseware as the default
  • Skills-taxonomy ownership — the shift from course completion to inferred skill level requires a maintained, structured skills taxonomy the system can reason against
  • Federated operating-model design — at least 70% of training is already localized to sales, manufacturing, and customer service rather than owned centrally
  • Succession and career-pathing judgment — the one part of this role's scope Bersin's research doesn't touch, and the data confirms stays almost entirely human
  • Business-case fluency — tying Level 4 maturity to Bersin's measurable outcomes (10x innovation leadership, 6x financial-target outperformance)

Framework connection: The competency list splits along the same line the Role Impact Profile shows — Knowledge-pattern activities (strategy, design, succession) call for the judgment-heavy skills above; the Transaction-pattern cluster is where Bersin's Dynamic Enablement model does the most direct replacing.
