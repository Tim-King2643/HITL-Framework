Architecture Runs Through the Whole Stack

Enterprise Architect's footprint spans 6 L3s across 3 of the domain's 7 process groups (40 L4 units) — from IT transformation guidance and enterprise architecture definition, through technology innovation management, to service/solution strategy, lifecycle planning, and architecture itself. That range matches what Research.com and TOGAF-focused career sources describe as the role's real scope: not confined to one layer of the technology stack, but responsible for aligning business, data, application, and technology architecture as one coherent whole. The mix stays Knowledge-dominant (70% of activities, M1 85% → M3 65%), consistent with a role built around systems analysis and judgment rather than hands-on execution — the architect designs the structure other roles build within.

Skills & Competencies

Grounded in Research.com's “2026 Enterprise Architect Careers: Skills, Education, Salary & Job Outlook” (June 2026).

  • Systems analysis and technology integration — understanding how components across the stack fit together, not just individually
  • Business capability analysis — translating business strategy into architectural structure that actually supports it
  • Cybersecurity and governance judgment — architecture decisions must reduce risk, not just enable capability
  • TOGAF and framework fluency — per Research.com, employers value certifications like TOGAF because they demonstrate familiarity with structured architecture methodology, and per PowerKram, TOGAF plus a cloud and AI-governance credential is now the profile associated with senior EA roles
  • Communication and stakeholder judgment — the best candidates can show how architecture decisions reduce risk, improve scalability, and support modernization in terms non-architects understand

Framework connection: This role's Knowledge-dominant mix (M1 85% → M3 65%) is the direct data expression of what Research.com calls judgment: architecture decisions require synthesizing constraints across business, data, application, and technology domains simultaneously — work that stays interpretive even as individual technical components become more automatable.

How the Role Has Evolved

1995
TAFIM Becomes TOGAF
The Open Group adapts the U.S. Department of Defense's Technical Architecture Framework for Information Management into TOGAF — the first vendor-neutral attempt to standardize enterprise architecture practice.
→
2002
TOGAF 8: The Full ADM Cycle
TOGAF 8 ("Enterprise Edition") introduces the complete Architecture Development Method — a structured, iterative process spanning business, data, application, and technology domains that remains the framework's methodological core today.
→
2009–11
Business Architecture Takes the Lead
TOGAF 9 shifts emphasis toward business architecture, deepening the link between strategic objectives and technical execution. TOGAF 9.1 (2011) becomes the most widely adopted version in the framework's history.
→
2022
TOGAF 10 Modernizes
After years of TOGAF 9.2 failing to keep pace with cloud, agility, and digital transformation, TOGAF 10 restructures the standard — cutting it from 692 to 532 pages and modularizing content that changes quickly from content that stays stable.
→
2026 ★
AI Governance Becomes Core EA Work
Per PowerKram's 2026 career analysis, AI governance has rapidly become a core Enterprise Architecture responsibility — TOGAF 10 is now explicitly positioned to help organizations architect for agentic AI, not just traditional IT systems.

Era framing follows the AIHR “HR Business Partner Guide” infographic's own historical structure; TOGAF version history verified across multiple independent sources (CIO.com, CIO Wiki, The Open Group's own published standard), not reproduced from any single article's text.

A related pattern: McKinsey's Julie Goran, writing in July 2026, frames the scale of what's changing as comparable to the dawn of the Industrial Age — leaders are now asking whether AI agents belong on the org chart itself, and how to plan spans and layers around agent capacity alongside human capacity. For Enterprise Architect, that's now a literal TOGAF 10 use case: this role's own Knowledge-heavy work (M1 85% → M3 65%) is exactly the systems-level judgment TOGAF's ADM was built to structure — now being extended to structure where agents fit in the architecture too.
