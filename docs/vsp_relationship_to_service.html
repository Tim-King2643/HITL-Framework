Description

This value stream comprises one APQC PCF process group: 8.1 Develop and Manage IT Customer Relationships — spanning 7 Level 3 processes and 36 Level 4 activities.

At the activity level, this value stream's work breaks down as 23 Knowledge activities (judgment, synthesis, and interpretation-driven work), 6 Transaction activities (rules-based processing suited to automation), 3 Decision activities (judgment-and-authority gates that stay human by design), 3 Document activities (content generation), and 1 Exception activity (non-standard resolution).

At M3 (HITL Design Intent), the value stream's aggregate human-involvement ratio is 60%, down from an 81% undesigned baseline — a 21-point AI convergence opportunity.

Design Narrative

Relationship-to-Service concentrates ownership more tightly than its former combined form: IT Service Manager holds Accountable for 5 of the stream's 7 L3s — understanding customer needs (8.1.1), service planning and communication (8.1.3), service-level definition (8.1.5), ongoing relationship management (8.1.6), and performance analysis (8.1.7) — the day-to-day ITSM lane this stream is built around. IT Director holds the two remaining L3s, both explicitly transformation-facing rather than operational: identifying IT customer transformation needs (8.1.2) and providing transformation guidance (8.1.4), where Enterprise Architect and CIO appear only as Consulted, never Accountable. Human judgment concentrates in negotiation and prioritization — SLA negotiation (8.1.5.6) and IT-opportunity prioritization (8.1.4.4, 8.1.4.6) are the stream's three Decision-pattern activities. Its steepest AI-absorption opportunity sits in 8.1.3.4 (Create IT marketing messages, Document-pattern, -32 points).

Handoff Points

The stream opens with IT Service Manager on understanding customer needs (8.1.1) and service planning/communication (8.1.3), then briefly hands to IT Director for the two transformation-facing L3s (8.1.2 transformation needs, 8.1.4 transformation guidance) before returning to IT Service Manager.

IT Service Manager closes the stream: service-level definition and negotiation (8.1.5), ongoing relationship management (8.1.6), and performance analysis (8.1.7) — the stream's largest single lane.

IT Support Specialist appears at both ends as Responsible (8.1.1 needs assessment, 8.1.7 performance analysis) without ever holding Accountable — a data-gathering and execution role bookending IT Service Manager's ownership.
